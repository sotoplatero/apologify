A good apology letter does three things: it names exactly what you did, shows you understand how it landed, and says what will change. Below is the format, a template you can fill in, and real examples — built on what we learned from [845 real apologies](/research/why-people-apologize).

## The apology letter format, in five parts

### 1. Say sorry, plainly

Open with the apology itself, not with context. "I'm sorry" or "I apologize" in the first line tells them this letter is about them, not about you.

> *I'm sorry for what I said to you on Saturday.*

### 2. Name exactly what you did

This is the part most apologies skip. In our study, 12.3% of people couldn't name the act at all — "sorry for the misunderstanding." Say the specific thing, in the words they'd use.

> *In front of your sister, I said you never finish anything. That was cruel, and it wasn't true.*

### 3. Show you understand the impact

Describe what it did to them, without arguing about whether it should have. This is what makes someone feel heard.

> *You'd been nervous about that dinner all week, and I made you feel small at the one moment you needed me on your side.*

### 4. Take responsibility — no "but"

Explanations are fine; excuses aren't. If a sentence starts with "but" or "if you felt," cut it.

> *I was stressed about work, but that's mine to deal with. It was never yours to absorb.*

### 5. Say what you'll do differently

A concrete change or repair, small enough to keep. Then let them respond in their own time — don't demand forgiveness.

> *Next time I'm on edge, I'll say so instead of taking it out on you. Take whatever time you need.*

## Most apologies are about words, not deeds

We classified 845 real apologies by what people were sorry for. **38.4% came down to something said** — a hurtful remark, a lost temper, disrespect. 26% involved a phone: ignored texts, going silent, blocking. Forgetting a birthday or anniversary was only 1.2%.

The lesson for your letter: quote the moment. The more exactly you name it, the more it reads as real. [Read the full study](/research/why-people-apologize).

## Apology letter template

Replace everything in brackets. If a bracket feels hard to fill, that's the part the letter needs most.

```text
Dear [Name],

I'm sorry for [what you did — one specific sentence].

[When and where it happened, briefly.] I know it [how it affected them:
hurt you, embarrassed you, made you feel ignored].

There's no excuse for it. [Optional: one honest line of context — without "but."]

From now on, I'm going to [one concrete change or repair].

I understand if you need time. Whenever you're ready, I'd like to talk.

[Your name]
```

## Apology letter examples

### To a partner — for going silent after an argument

> Dear Sam,
>
> I'm sorry I went silent on you after our argument on Thursday. I saw every one of your messages and chose not to answer for two days.
>
> I know that left you wondering whether we were okay, and that waiting felt worse than the argument itself. Going quiet felt like cooling off to me; to you it felt like being shut out.
>
> From now on, if I need space, I'll say "I need a few hours, I'm not going anywhere" instead of disappearing.
>
> I love you, and I'm here whenever you want to talk.
>
> Alex

### To a friend — for missing their birthday dinner

> Hi Jordan,
>
> I'm sorry I didn't show up to your birthday dinner on Friday — and that I didn't even text to say so. You found out I wasn't coming from an empty chair.
>
> You'd booked the table for everyone, and I made a night that was supposed to be about you about my absence. That's not what a friend does.
>
> I'd like to take you out this week, just us, my treat — whenever works for you.
>
> Maya

### To a teacher — for disrupting class

> Dear Ms. Carter,
>
> I apologize for my behavior in class on Tuesday. I kept talking while you were explaining the assignment, and when you asked me to stop, I answered back in a rude way.
>
> That interrupted the lesson for everyone and was disrespectful to you in front of the class.
>
> From now on I'll stay focused during instruction, and if I disagree with something, I'll ask to speak with you after class.
>
> Sincerely,\
> Daniel Reyes

## What not to write

- **"I'm sorry if you were hurt."** "If" puts the hurt in question. Say "I'm sorry I hurt you."
- **"I'm sorry, but…"** Everything after "but" erases what came before it.
- **"Sorry for the misunderstanding."** It names nothing. Say what actually happened.
- **Three paragraphs about your week.** Context is one line. The letter is about them.
- **"Please forgive me."** Asking is fine; demanding a reply isn't. Leave them room.
- **Bringing up what they did.** That's a different conversation. Have it another day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you start an apology letter?

Start with the apology itself — "I'm sorry for…" followed by the specific thing you did. Skip warm-ups like "I've been thinking a lot lately." The first sentence should tell them exactly what this letter is about.

### How long should an apology letter be?

Usually 100–250 words: long enough to name what happened, how it affected them and what you'll change; short enough that it doesn't turn into a story about you. A formal apology at work can be even shorter.

### How do you end an apology letter?

End with what you'll do differently and an open door — "I'd like to talk whenever you're ready." Don't end by asking them to forgive you right away. For formal letters, close with "Sincerely" and your full name.
