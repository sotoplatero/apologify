Most apology letters to a girlfriend fail the same way: they say "I'm sorry for everything" and never name the thing. In [our study](/research/why-people-apologize) of 484 real apologies to a partner, the top reasons were saying something hurtful (15.3%), not being there for them (13.6%), going silent (11.2%) and losing your temper (10.7%) — and 14.5% never said what happened at all. Here's how to write one that doesn't.

## How to write it

1. **Start with the apology.** "I'm sorry for what I said on Friday" — not three paragraphs about how much you love her first.
2. **Name exactly what you did.** Quote the words, the night, the missed call. Specific is what makes it believable.
3. **Show you understand how it felt to her.** Embarrassed in front of friends, ignored, second to your phone. Say it from her side, not yours.
4. **Own it — no "but," no "if."** "I'm sorry if you were upset" puts it back on her. One line of context is fine; an excuse isn't.
5. **Say what changes, then give her room.** One concrete thing you'll do differently. Don't demand an answer tonight.

## Sample letters

### For saying something hurtful in a fight

> Emma,
>
> I'm sorry for what I said on Sunday. When we were arguing about the trip, I told you that you always make everything about yourself. That isn't true, and I said it because I knew it would hurt.
>
> You'd been planning that weekend for weeks, and I turned it into a reason to make you feel selfish.
>
> I was frustrated about money, but that's mine to sort out, not yours to pay for. Next time I feel myself getting there, I'm going to stop and say I need ten minutes instead of saying something I can't take back.
>
> I love you. Whenever you're ready to talk, I'm here.
>
> Ryan

### For not being there when she needed you

> Hey Sophie,
>
> I'm sorry I wasn't there on Thursday. You told me your interview went badly and I said "that sucks" and went back to the game. You called me afterwards and I let it ring.
>
> You needed someone to listen and I made you feel like you were bothering me. That's the opposite of what I want to be for you.
>
> I'd like to take you to dinner this weekend — no phone, just us — and hear the whole thing properly. And from now on, when you call on a hard day, I pick up.
>
> Josh

### For going silent after an argument

> Maya,
>
> I'm sorry I stopped answering after our fight on Tuesday. I saw your messages and left them on read for a whole day.
>
> To me it felt like cooling off. To you it must have felt like I was punishing you, or like I was gone. You shouldn't have had to wonder.
>
> If I need space again, I'll tell you: "I need a few hours, I'm not going anywhere." I'm not going anywhere.
>
> Dan

### Short version to text her

> I've been thinking about last night. I'm really sorry for snapping at you in front of your friends — you didn't deserve that, and I was embarrassed after. Can I call you when you're free?

## What not to write

- **"I'm sorry you feel that way."** It apologizes for her feelings, not your actions.
- **"I'm sorry, but you also…"** Her part is a separate conversation, on a separate day.
- **"I'll never do it again."** Too big to believe. Promise one specific change you can keep.
- **A love letter instead of an apology.** Saying how much she means to you is fine at the end; it can't replace naming what you did.
- **"Please forgive me, I can't sleep."** Your guilt isn't her job. Let her take her time.

## Before you send it

- Wait until you're calm — but not a week. A day or two is usually right.
- Write it as you'd say it. If it sounds like a greeting card, rewrite it.
- If it was big, follow the letter with a conversation in person.
- Then show it. The next time the same situation comes up is the real apology.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I write a sorry letter to my girlfriend after a big fight?

Wait until you're both calm, then apologize for your part specifically — the thing you said or did, not "the fight." Say how it landed for her, what you'll do differently, and offer to talk when she's ready.

### Should I text or write a letter?

For small things, a sincere text and a call is enough. For something that really hurt her — cruel words, a broken promise, disappearing — a letter shows you took time to understand it. You can send it on paper, by email, or as a page she opens on her phone.

### How long should an apology letter to my girlfriend be?

Usually 150–250 words. Long enough to name what happened and how it affected her; short enough that it doesn't become a story about you.

### What if she doesn't answer?

Give her time. One gentle message after a few days is fine; pushing for forgiveness undoes the apology.
