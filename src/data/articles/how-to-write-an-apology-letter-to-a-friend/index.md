Friendships rarely end over one mistake; they end over the apology that never came. In [our study](/research/why-people-apologize) of 219 real apologies to friends, the top reasons were saying something hurtful (21.5%), lying or breaking trust (14.6%) and going silent (10%). Here's how to write yours.

## How to write it

1. **Talk like yourself.** Use the voice you use with them. A stiff, formal letter reads as distance.
2. **Name the moment.** "What I said about your job at Sara's party" — not "everything that happened."
3. **Say what it cost them.** Embarrassment, feeling betrayed, feeling dropped. Show you see it from their side.
4. **Own it, no "but".** One line of context is fine. Turning it into their fault is not.
5. **Offer a next step.** Coffee, a call, a promise you can keep. Then give them room to answer.

## Sample letters

### For saying something hurtful

> Hey Priya,
>
> I'm sorry for what I said at Jake's on Saturday. Joking that you'd "never actually finish" your degree in front of everyone wasn't a joke — it was mean, and it touched exactly what you'd told me you were worried about.
>
> You trusted me with that, and I used it to get a laugh.
>
> I'm proud of how hard you're working, and I should have said that instead. Can I take you for coffee this week?
>
> Mia

### For sharing a secret

> Dear Ben,
>
> I told Chris about your interview before you were ready to tell anyone. I'm sorry. You asked me to keep it between us and I didn't.
>
> I know that made you feel like you can't tell me things, and I understand why.
>
> From now on, what you tell me stays with me. I'll prove that over time, not just in this letter.
>
> Sam

### For disappearing when they needed me

> Hi Taylor,
>
> I'm sorry I went quiet after your breakup. You reached out three times and I kept saying "I'll call you tomorrow."
>
> I was overwhelmed with my own stuff, but you needed a friend and I wasn't there. That must have felt lonely.
>
> I'm here now, and I'd love to see you this weekend if you're up for it.
>
> Jordan

### Short version to text a best friend

> Hey, I've been thinking about Friday. I'm really sorry for snapping at you in front of everyone — you didn't deserve that. Can we talk tonight?

## Before you send it

- Send it soon — a day or two after, not a month.
- If it was big, write it down; if it was small, a text and a call is enough.
- Don't list the things they did wrong too. That's a different conversation.
- Let them reply in their own time. Pushing for an answer undoes the apology.
- Then show up. The next time you see them matters more than the letter.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I apologize to my best friend after a big fight?

Give it a day so you're calm, then apologize for your part specifically — what you said or did — rather than for "the fight." A letter works well when talking feels too heated; follow it with an offer to meet.

### Should I text or write a letter to apologize to a friend?

For small things, a sincere text and a call is enough. For something that hurt them — a betrayed secret, cruel words, disappearing — a letter shows you took the time to think it through.

### What if my friend doesn't reply to my apology?

Give them time. You can send one gentle follow-up after a week or two, but don't keep pushing. A real apology doesn't come with a deadline for forgiveness.
