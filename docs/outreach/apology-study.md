## Los 3 titulares (úsalos siempre en este orden)

1. **38% of apologies are about words**: something hurtful, a lost temper, or disrespect. The #1 single reason is "said something hurtful" (17.2%).
2. **1 in 4 apologies involves a phone** (26%): ignoring texts, going silent, blocking. 29% between partners.
3. **The clichés barely exist**: forgetting a birthday or anniversary is just 1.2%; money, 0.8%.

Bonus: 12% couldn't name what they did ("it was a misunderstanding").

Siempre que lo cites, menciona los límites: muestra autoseleccionada de usuarios de una herramienta online, clasificación con IA. Ser transparente es lo que hace que un periodista se fíe.

## 3. Hilo en X

Enlace: `https://apologify.com/research/why-people-apologize?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=apology-study`

1. We read 845 real apologies to find out what people actually say sorry for. The answer isn't what greeting cards think. 🧵 [imagen del gráfico]
2. #1 reason: saying something hurtful (17%). Add losing your temper and being disrespectful and 38% of all apologies come down to words — not deeds.
3. 1 in 4 apologies involves a phone: ignoring texts, going silent, blocking. Between partners it's 29%. Silence is now its own kind of hurt.
4. The sitcom apology — forgetting a birthday or anniversary — is just 1.2%. Money: 0.8%. Real apologies are about everyday moments.
5. And 12% couldn't name what they did. "Sorry for the misunderstanding" is the apology most likely to land as hollow.
